Reflection

Our Community-Engaged Learning Reflection

This reflection explains how our VIDIDIT project connects technology, education, and community improvement. It includes our initial thoughts before beginning the project and our final reflection after developing a clearer understanding of the community need.

Initial Reflection

What We Think Community-Engaged Learning Means

Community-engaged learning means using academic knowledge to understand and respond to real problems faced by people in society. It is not only about completing a project, but about connecting classroom learning with meaningful community needs. In our project, this means using web development and AI-related ideas to address an educational challenge: helping students move from simply receiving answers to actually understanding concepts.

Our Experience with Community Service

As a group, we understand community service as any meaningful contribution that supports others through time, knowledge, effort, or problem-solving. Even if our experiences are different, this project gives us an opportunity to contribute through education and technology by designing a website that presents a solution for students, teachers, and learning communities.

How We Feel About Working on This Project

We feel that working on a community-engagement project is valuable because it gives our technical work a larger purpose. Instead of creating a website only for design practice, we are developing a platform idea that can support learning, critical thinking, and responsible AI use. This makes the project more meaningful because it connects technology with human development.

Engineering Design Process and Community Improvement

The engineering design process helps people solve problems by first identifying a need, researching the issue, planning a solution, creating a prototype, testing it, and improving it based on feedback. For VIDIDIT, this process helps us understand the learning gap, study existing AI tools, design a better educational solution, and present it through a structured website.

Information We Need

To strengthen this project, we need information about current AI learning tools, student learning challenges, educator feedback, market risks, competitor platforms, and research evidence on explainable AI and personalized education. This information helps us make VIDIDIT more realistic, useful, and connected to actual community needs.

Final Reflection

Strengths We Discovered

During this project, we discovered strengths in teamwork, problem analysis, research, creativity, and web design. We also learned that we could connect technical skills with a real educational purpose. The project helped us think beyond coding and focus on how technology can support understanding and learning.

Challenges We Faced

One challenge was organizing different ideas into a clear website structure. Since the project included community engagement, market analysis, reflection, and technical design, we had to divide the content carefully across separate pages. We also had to make sure the website was not only visually appealing but also meaningful and aligned with the project requirements.

Qualities We Want to Improve

We learned that we would like to improve our planning, communication, time management, and ability to collect real-world feedback earlier in the project. We also want to become better at explaining technical ideas in a simple and accessible way for different audiences.

Memorable Learning from Real-World Partners and Organizations

Through our research, we learned that educational organizations and technology platforms are increasingly focusing on AI-supported learning. However, many tools still focus mainly on giving quick answers or generating content. This made us realize that VIDIDIT should focus more strongly on explainability, reasoning, and student understanding.

What Surprised Us About the People Served

One important realization was that students may have access to more information than ever before, but that does not automatically mean they understand better. Many learners need guidance, explanation, and confidence-building rather than only fast answers. This helped us see education as a community issue, not just a classroom issue.

One Thing We Wish More People Knew

We wish more people understood that AI in education should not replace thinking. It should support thinking. A strong AI learning tool should help students ask better questions, understand reasoning, and become more independent learners.
